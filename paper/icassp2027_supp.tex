\textbf{Measures.} Two quantities are easy to conflate and are kept apart throughout.
A \emph{success rate} is a rate, the fraction of continuations the estimator assigns to
the target key within the likelihood-change threshold. A \emph{patching gain} is a
difference of two such rates, the replacement's minus its random control's at the same
layer, so a patching gain and a success rate are not comparable. Two random controls are
used. The dimension-matched control replaces the same number of randomly chosen
directions ($24$), and the displacement-matched control additionally matches the
activation displacement. Every per-key test, every layer scan and every patching gain is
measured against the dimension-matched control ($0.0391$ in major, $0.0027$ in minor),
while the main text's abstract reports the displacement-matched one.

This supplement records the protocol in enough detail to reproduce it and reports the
control experiments and additional analyses that the main text summarizes. The table
below lists the question each section answers, including the analyses whose results
weaken our claims.

\textbf{D-SYN}: $200{,}000$ training, $10{,}000$ validation and $10{,}000$
test pieces from a functional-harmony generator, plus a separate $200{,}000/10{,}000$
pair for the reference model. The four sets share no piece: hashing every token
sequence gives zero overlap between train and test, train and reference-train, and
test and either reference set, so the reference model's ``data the other models never
see'' is disjoint by content and not merely by filename.

\textbf{Vocabulary and piece length.} The $124$ token types are $88$ absolute MIDI
pitches ($21$--$108$), $16$ beat positions, $16$ note lengths, one bar mark, and the
three structural tokens \texttt{PAD}, \texttt{BOS} and \texttt{EOS}. There is no key
and no chord symbol, and no token whose identity depends on a key. A piece runs
$12$--$22$ bars, median $17$, which is $278$--$508$ tokens, median $393$.

\textbf{Where transposition is and is not used.} Three different things could be
called transposition here, and only the last changes what a model sees at training
time.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Training augmentation (D-SYN only).} The R-Aug regime draws one uniform
transposition per sequence in token-id space at each step; R-NoAug draws none. The
corpus on disk is identical for both, so the six models differ in seed and in this
augmentation, not in data.
\item \emph{Estimating the targets.} The per-key means come from a key-balanced
sample. On Bach that sample is built by transposing each chorale through all twelve
tonics, because the $300$ chorales contain no F$\sharp$ major and no D$\sharp$ minor
at all and a direct mean would leave those two targets as zero vectors. The split is
by original chorale, so a piece's transpositions never straddle train and test.
\item \emph{The transposition reference.} Transposing the prompt itself is a
behavioural reference condition, not a training or estimation step (Table~1).
\end{itemize}
No public checkpoint is fine-tuned, retrained, or augmented: we only read and edit
their activations.

\textbf{Bach chorales.} $300$ chorales in \texttt{**kern}, keys from the
Roman-numeral analyses of \emph{When in Rome}. A chorale is matched to its analysis by
number, never by order.

\textbf{POP909-CL.} $909$ files, $905$ load. Four are excluded by a rule fixed before
evaluation, with reasons recorded: two carry no key-signature event at all, and two are
listed by the release as having misaligned downbeats and algorithmic rather than expert
labels. $127$ pieces change key. Pieces split $0.7/0.15/0.15$ into train (probe,
per-key means, threshold), search (layer and hyperparameter choices) and final
(touched once); a piece lands wholly in one part.

\textbf{Piece exclusions from a model's window.} Each tokenization bounds
representable time, and a piece is excluded when fewer than $34$ note positions fall
inside that bound. This removes exactly one piece (\texttt{247}, which opens with a
$70.5$-beat silence), for the REMI checkpoint only, in the train split only. The
held-out splits lose nothing.

Multinomial logistic probe per layer, trained with AdamW; a two-layer MLP as a
secondary probe. Splits are by sequence, so no position of a piece appears in two
parts. The note-counting baselines (C3) look back over windows of $8$, $16$, $32$ and
$64$ pitch tokens and predict the key from the twelve pitch-class counts, by logistic
regression and by Krumhansl--Schmuckler. The control task (C1b) permutes the key
labels per sequence, giving the floor that is subtracted.

The pre-specified baseline peaked at the edge of its window sweep, so it was extended
with a wider, concatenated window, and the strongest of all of them
(\texttt{lr\_W16cat512}, $F_1$ $0.824$, against the pre-specified
\texttt{lr\_W64}'s $0.790$) is what the reported margin is measured against.

At layer $4$ the probe reaches macro-$F_1$ $0.927$ against that $0.824$; the control
floor is $0.032$, leaving $+0.071$ $[0.056,0.081]$. Against the pre-specified window
set alone the same layer gives $+0.105$ $[0.091,0.114]$, so widening the counter's
history costs margin without overturning the result. An untrained network of the same
shape reaches $0.243$, and the raw probe score is flat across layers $2$--$6$
($0.920$--$0.929$) while layer $1$ reaches $0.872$ and layer $0$ only $0.637$.

\textbf{The two layers nearest the input are exceptions.} Against the strongest
baseline the margin clears zero in layers $2$--$7$ of all six models (smallest lower
bound $+0.021$), but not at layer $0$ ($-0.231$) or layer $1$ ($+0.012$, CI
$[-0.00006, +0.021]$). Layer $1$ fails in three of the six models
(R-Aug\_s0, R-Aug\_s2, R-NoAug\_s1) and clears zero in the other three, so we treat
it as unresolved rather than positive. Against the pre-specified baseline alone layer
$1$ would clear zero in all six ($+0.044$ to $+0.062$). The layer-$1$ verdict is the
one conclusion in this paper that depends on which baseline is used.

\textbf{Gate} (before any intervention work): validation next-token top-$1$ must reach
$1.5\times$ the constant-predictor rate of $0.434$, and the pitch-in-scale rate of
unconditional generation must stay within $3$ standard deviations of the validation
mean. All $13$ finished checkpoints pass; the six main models measure
$0.878$--$0.881$.

\textbf{Budget} $\delta_{\text{PPL}}$, frozen before any edit is scored, as the
$90$th percentile of the reference model's perplexity rise across natural key changes:
$0.613$ over $7{,}989$ events on D-SYN. The public-model thresholds are per corpus, since
a threshold in one model's nats does not transfer: $0.849$ on Bach over $1{,}180$ events
(reference \texttt{music-medium-800k}) and $1.119$ on POP909 over $103$ events
(reference \texttt{music-large-800k}). A run whose frozen threshold names a different
reference model is a hard error, not a warning.

The ordering never changes. From $\tau=0.2$, where $0.424$ of edited continuations

replacement's position is untouched throughout.

One row is flat, and it is the informative one: writing at pitch positions only scores
$0.035$ in major and $0.000$ in minor at every threshold, because it barely changes the
music and so never pays the threshold. The position null of Section~\ref{sec:position} is
therefore not an artefact of the scoring rule.

The comparison against steering is threshold-independent in the form the paper claims
it. At matched displacement the replacement wins at every threshold in both modes: in
major from $0.241$ against $0.176$ at $\tau=0.2$ to $0.410$ against $0.267$ with no

text already reports, while in minor the two cross near the frozen threshold, at $0.505$

threshold and with no limit are almost the same ($0.540$ and $0.540$ for AMT-24L,

wide margin in every cell at every threshold.

summary when the threshold was frozen, so only $p50$, $p75$, $p90$ and $p99$ exist as

validation split, which we did not do, and the threshold grid is the wider statement in
any case. And one condition cannot be re-scored at all: the transposition reference
carries no likelihood-change value, because its unedited twin is untransposed and the
criterion is undefined for it.

\textbf{Labels against notes.} Before any activation was read, the POP909 labels were
checked by estimating the key of every labelled segment of $\geq16$ notes and
comparing: thresholds \emph{exact} $\geq0.40$ and \emph{near} $\geq0.75$ were committed
first. Result over $1{,}060$ segments: exact $0.769$, near $0.958$; chance for exact is
$1/24=0.042$.

\textbf{Tokenizer against the model.} Each public model's token scheme is
re-implemented from its published configuration, so before probing we check that the
model finds correctly encoded music cheaper than corrupted music. The gate detects a
wrong field structure and a wrong time scale. It does not detect a uniform offset on
the note field, because in these vocabularies that is a transposition: the note codes
are instrument-major (verified against the reference implementation, which masks one
instrument as $128$ consecutive codes), so an off-by-one moves pitch and could reach
the instrument field only at pitch $127$, which never occurs in the $1.94$M notes used
here. That case is covered instead by two direct checks: all $15$ vocabulary constants
match the official implementation and the token streams agree on five real pieces of
$\approx600$ tokens; and the corpus conversion round-trips with every pitch and every
note preserved, onsets moving by at most one grid step ($5$\,ms absolute-time,
$25$\,ms beat-grid).

\textbf{Sampling.} The reference sampler masks the token families that do not belong in
the current slot and forbids control and special codes; ours draws from the whole
vocabulary, deliberately, so that no hand-written grammar can flatter the edit.
Measured on unedited continuations: not one generated note of $1{,}876$ left the
prompt's instrument block, and slot violations are $2.40\%$ on Bach and $0\%$ on pop,
all of them control or special codes, concentrated in one prompt of twelve.

\textbf{One estimate per continuation.} The estimator is applied once to the whole
continuation, not per token: the pitches are pooled into a single pitch-class
histogram of $110$ notes at the median, truncated at the end-of-piece token and after
sixteen bars, then correlated against the twenty-four rotated profiles, and the argmax is
the continuation's key. Success is that key matching the target key exactly, in both
tonic and mode; there is no partial credit for a neighbouring key.

This granularity is a deliberate choice, and its cost lowers the reported rate. A
continuation that needs a bar or two to settle into the target key carries the
prompt's key in its histogram for those bars, and the pooled estimate is diluted by
them; a continuation that modulates part-way through gets one label rather than two.
The measure is therefore conservative about onset, which is part of why the tonic
metrics of \S\ref{sec:tonic}, which read only how the music ends, give much higher
numbers on the same continuations ($0.755$ against $0.355$). Where bar-level resolution
is what the question needs, we measure per bar instead: the persistence rows of
\S\ref{sec:oneshot} carry an estimate for each bar.

Our Krumhansl--Schmuckler implementation was checked against \texttt{music21}. The
Krumhansl--Kessler profiles are identical in both modes, and given the same pitch
content with uniform note lengths, so that duration weighting and unweighted counts
coincide, the two implementations return the same key on $800$ of $800$ real
segments, with no disagreement of any relation.

For the public models: $20$ search prompts, $60$ held-out prompts, $12$ major targets,
$240$ new tokens, rank $24$, temperature $1.0$, top-$p$ $0.95$, seed $0$. MMT uses
$80$ steps instead of $240$, declared in advance, because its compound scheme emits one
position per note where the flat scheme emits three tokens.

Before any edit runs, a sham edit that projects out the subspace and re-inserts the
same component must reproduce the clean continuation token for token; it is computed
as a real edit rather than short-circuited, so a detached hook or a drifted position
makes it fail.

\textbf{Per-layer separation} (search stage, our model). These are patching gains, that is
differences of success rates against the dimension-matched control at the same layer,
and not success rates: the replacement beats its control by $0.030$ and $0.044$ at
layers $0$ and $1$, then by $0.193$, $0.258$, $0.303$, $0.259$, $0.232$ and $0.228$ at
layers $2$--$7$. The key is readable at layers $0$--$1$, where writing to it barely
works. The main text's Fig.~3 plots these gains, and its layer-$4$ value of $0.303$ is a
gain over that control on the $100$ search prompts, not the final test's success rate of
$0.3555$.

\textbf{Estimation condition.} Per-key targets come from a key-balanced sample, built
by transposing each piece through all twelve keys and splitting by original piece so
transpositions never straddle. On Bach this is forced: no F$\sharp$ major and no
D$\sharp$ minor occur, so a direct estimate leaves those two targets as zero vectors
and the F$\sharp$ major cell scores exactly $0$: edit $0.365$ with $11/12$ keys
separating for the $12$-layer model, and $0.439$ with $11/12$ for the $24$-layer one,
against $0.479$ and $0.557$ balanced.

\textbf{A prediction that was not borne out.} The balanced re-estimation for AMT
$\times$ pop was specified in advance with its expected effect written down: ``$0.135$
becomes $\approx0.17$, which likely still fails the per-key bar. The run happens either
way, and its outcome is reported either way.'' It reached $0.212$ with $9/12$ keys
separating (the three misses at $p_{\text{Holm}}$ $0.051$--$0.052$) and the
pitch-in-scale rates crossing. The prediction was wrong, and the cell is no longer read
as a failure.

% 加算は置換を追い越す（0.471 対 0.355）。これは事前指定した方向とは逆であり、出たままを
% 報告する。加算はその追加の押しの代償を、4倍では継続の半分が尤度変化の閾値を破ることで、
% 8倍ではほぼ全部が破ることで支払う。平均差の方向はプロンプトの調をほとんどどこにも
% 残さない（継続の 0.6%、音階内音高率 0.438）。これは古い成分の除去が、減算によってでは
% なく暗黙に起きているということである。短調では、変位を揃えた加算はさらにずっと弱い
% （0.154 対 置換の 0.495、12調すべてが有意）。
% つまり加算でも調を設定することはできる。置換が買うのは摂動あたりの効率であり、この
% 比較が単離しているのは、既に書かれているものを除去することが仕事の一部を担っている、
% ということである。

The pre-specified comparison substitutes an addition of the same value for the
replacement of Eq.~(3), so the only difference left is whether the old component $P_Vh$
is removed, which addition provably keeps. Held-out prompts are paired by prompt against
the replacement rows.

At matched displacement, replacement wins by $0.111$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.078,0.145]$, three
of twelve keys individually significant after correction), and the notes show the
mechanism: $28.8\%$ of the addition's continuations stay in the prompt's key, against
$11.5\%$ for the doubled addition and $0.6\%$ for the contrastive direction. Given
twice the displacement the addition overtakes replacement ($0.471$ against $0.355$),
which is the opposite of the pre-specified direction and is reported as observed; it
pays for the extra push at $4\times$, where half the continuations break the
threshold, and at $8\times$, where nearly all do. The difference-of-means direction leaves
the prompt key almost nowhere ($0.6\%$ of continuations, pitch-in-scale rate $0.438$), which
is the removal of the old component happening implicitly rather than by subtraction. In
minor keys the displacement-matched addition is weaker still ($0.154$ against the
replacement's $0.495$,
all twelve keys significant).

Addition can therefore set a key as well. What replacement provides is efficiency per
unit of perturbation, and what the comparison isolates is that removing what is already
written does part of the work.

same displacement. It does not answer which operation changes the music less at the same
effect, and that needs a curve rather than a point. The curve already exists: the

per-continuation row for each cell, so success and likelihood change can both be read
back per $\alpha$ with no generation. What follows is therefore a characterisation on
the $100$ search prompts, not a headline, and it changes no reported number. The
displacement is not stored per row but is fixed by construction at
$\alpha\,\bar{s}(\ell)$.

operates, the addition never reaches it at any displacement in the sweep: its best cell
scores $0.219$ against the replacement's $0.402$, and pushing harder makes it worse rather
than better. At layer~$2$, which the addition's own search chose, matched displacement
again favours the replacement ($0.276$ against $0.155$), but $1.353\times$ the displacement
is enough for the addition to catch it and twice the displacement puts it ahead
($0.497$).

Read in nats rather than in displacement the picture changes, and this result weakens
our claim. At layer~$2$ with $\alpha=2$ the addition is not only more successful but also
changes the likelihood less than the replacement, a median of $-0.059$ against $0.162$,
meaning the reference model finds those continuations no more surprising than the
unedited ones. Therefore the
replacement is the more efficient operation per unit of the quantity we control, the
displacement, and not per unit of the quantity we measure as damage. Why the replacement
is expensive at layer~$2$ is geometric: the displacement there is $20.345$ against a mean
activation norm of $49.733$, so it moves $41\%$ of the activation, where at layer~$4$ it
moves $21\%$ of a much longer vector.

One further property of the addition is visible in the same table. It has no fixed
point, so raising $\alpha$ eventually destroys the distribution rather than
strengthening the effect: the median likelihood change reaches thousands of nats
($24{,}015$ at $\alpha=8$ on layer~$2$, $86{,}216$ at $\alpha=16$) while the success rate
collapses. The replacement cannot do this, because by construction it cannot overshoot the
value it writes.

\section{The value written is appropriate to the position \normalfont\textit{(main Sec.~4.4)}}
\label{sec:position}

The per-key means pool the token types, since of $144{,}000$ sampled positions $62{,}509$
are pitch and $69{,}134$ bar or note length, and the edit then writes the same
$P_V\mu_\kappa$ wherever the mask allows. That invites an objection to the position
result: editing only pitch positions may do nothing because the model does not consult
the key there, or because we wrote a value that does not belong there.

Estimating $\mu_\kappa$ from each family on its own separates the two. Inside $V$,
the two estimates of the same key agree to a cosine of $0.920$ on average
($0.877$--$0.953$) and are the same size (norm ratio $1.028$, $0.936$--$1.097$). For
scale, two different keys estimated from the same family agree only to $0.314$ on
average, at most $0.808$, so the worst-matched key across families is still more
aligned than any two distinct keys are within one. The value we write is therefore
close to position-independent, and the null result at pitch positions is not an
artefact of writing an alien target.

This settles the value; it does not settle the subspace. $V$ comes from a probe
trained on pooled positions too, so a position-specific $V$ remains a separate
question we did not test.

\section{The edit reaches distant keys \normalfont\textit{(main Sec.~4.2)}}
\label{sec:distance}
% 【日本語訳】編集は遠い調にも届くか（本文4.2節）
% 近隣の調の間でしか働かない置換は弱い結果になる。どの調も置換られていないまま、
% モデルが五度圏を1歩だけ押されただけかもしれないからである。そこで取り置きの全行を、
% プロンプトの調と目標調の五度圏距離 d で分割した。100本のプロンプトはそれぞれ
% 同一調を除く11標的すべてで評価されるので、行は構成上すでに対になっている。
% d=1..5 に各200行、五度圏で唯一の調である三全音の d=6 に100行である。
% 数値を見る前に、それぞれの結果が何を意味するかを固定した。距離係数の信頼区間がゼロを
% 含めば、編集は距離に依存せず、主張は述べた以上に強い。係数が負で、それでも d=6 が
% 統制を明確に上回るなら、効果は本物だが減衰する。d=5 と d=6 で統制から分離しないなら、
% 成功率は近い調に担われていることになり、主張の射程を狭めねばならない。
% 成り立つのは第1の場合であり、両旋法でそうなる。プロンプトをクラスタとして成功を d に
% 回帰すると（GEE、交換可能、ロジスティック）、長調プロンプトで β_d = −0.004、

% 当てはめと、プロンプト単位のクラスタブートストラップ2000反復も一致し、3つすべてを

% （短調）で成功し、変位を揃えた統制は 0.000 と 0.030 である。

% 調であるときに 0.195 で成功し、それより遠いところではどこでも 0.035 以下になる。これは、
% 推定器がもともとその標的を名指ししかけていたときにだけ成功として登録される介入の
% プロファイルである。置換にはそのプロファイルが無い。
% 尤度変化の閾値が遠い置換を制限しているのではないかも検討した。閾値は自然な転調から
% 凍結しており、自然な転調は近い調を好むので、遠い標的を構造的に不利にする閾値は距離の
% 効果を捏造しうる。各失敗を「推定器が標的を名指ししなかった」「閾値を超えた」「両方」に
% 分けると、長調プロンプトでの閾値だけによる失敗率は d に対して平坦である（0.050、
% 0.060、0.035、0.050、0.065、0.080）。この仮説は支持されない。失敗は全域にわたって
% 推定器が標的を名指ししないことに支配されている（0.405 から 0.540）。短調プロンプトでは
% 閾値だけの率が距離とともに緩やかに上昇し、0.115 から 0.170 になるので、プールした数字
% ではなくそれを報告する。
%

A replacement that only worked between neighbouring keys would be a weak result: the
model might be nudged a step around the circle of fifths without a key being
replaced. We therefore split every held-out row by the circle-of-fifths distance $d$
between the prompt's key and the target key. Each of the $100$ prompts is evaluated
at all $11$ non-identity targets, so the rows are paired by construction: $200$ rows at
each of $d=1..5$ and $100$ at $d=6$, the tritone, where only one key lies.

Before reading the numbers we fixed what each outcome would mean. A confidence interval
on the distance coefficient that contains zero means the edit is distance-independent
and the claim is stronger than stated. A negative coefficient with $d=6$ still clearly
above the control means the effect is real but decays. No separation from the control at
$d=5$ and $6$ would mean the success rate is carried by near keys and the claim's reach
would have to be narrowed.

The first case holds, in both modes. Fitting success on $d$ with the prompt as a cluster
(GEE, exchangeable, logistic) gives $\hat\beta_d = -0.004$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.080, +0.072]$
for major prompts and $-0.046$, $[-0.116, +0.024]$ for minor. A mixed-effects fit and a
prompt-level cluster bootstrap over $2000$ replicates agree; all three are reported in
\texttt{results/reanalysis/a1\_a3/}. The tritone replacement succeeds at $0.340$ (major) and
$0.440$ (minor) against $0.000$ and $0.030$ for the displacement-matched control.

The control behaves in the opposite way. Its coefficient is strongly negative on major
prompts, $-0.689$, $[-0.937, -0.441]$: a random subspace succeeds at $0.195$ when the
target happens to be the neighbouring key and at $0.035$ or less everywhere beyond it.
That is the profile of an intervention that only ever registers as a success when the
estimator was nearly going to name the target anyway. The replacement does not have that
profile.

We also asked whether the likelihood-change threshold is what limits distant
replacements. It was frozen from natural key changes, and natural key changes favour
near keys, so a threshold that structurally penalised distant targets would manufacture
a distance effect. Splitting each failure into "the estimator did not name the target",
"the threshold was exceeded", and both, the threshold-only failure rate on major prompts
is flat in $d$ ($0.050$, $0.060$, $0.035$, $0.050$, $0.065$, $0.080$). The hypothesis is
not supported: failures are dominated throughout by the estimator not naming the target
($0.405$ to $0.540$). On minor prompts the threshold-only rate does rise mildly with
distance, from $0.115$ to $0.170$, which we report separately rather than pooling it.

\section{The geometry of the key subspace \normalfont\textit{(main Sec.~2.2)}}
\label{sec:geometry}

% その要約ではない。
%

$V$ is built from the probe's weights, so any structure inside it could be something the
probe imposed rather than something the model holds. The check is to measure the same
geometry on the raw class means $\mu_\kappa$, which the probe never touched.

Among major keys the circle of fifths is already there before any projection: the rank
correlation between cosine similarity and circle-of-fifths distance is $-0.936$ on the raw
means, $-0.961$ after removing the component common to all $24$ keys, and $-0.961$ after
projection into $V$. Projection sharpens the contrast, with neighbouring keys at $0.681$
and tritone-related keys at $-0.118$ against $0.978$ and $0.928$ before centring, but
it does not create the ordering. Classical scaling of the centred means recovers the
circle of fifths as a closed ring (Fig.~\ref{fig:supp-geometry}). Relative keys are closer
than parallel ones ($0.581$ against $0.435$), which we report without a prediction, since
theory does not settle which should be nearer.

Two facts point the other way. Among minor keys there is
essentially no fifths ordering ($-0.063$ centred, $-0.216$ projected), so the structure
described above is a property of the major keys. And $V$ captures only $5.2\%$ of the
energy of $\mu_\kappa$ on average: it is a thin slice of the class means, not a summary of
them.

$V$ comes from a probe trained on activations, which leaves the sharpest objection
standing. The subspace we edit might be nothing more than a linear image of the recent
pitch-class frequencies, that is, the note-counting baseline the probe beats by
$+0.071$ rewritten in other coordinates. If that held, the causal result would say only
that writing a pitch-class profile into the residual stream changes which pitches come
out, which is far less than the paper claims. The design and the reading of every
outcome, including the outcomes that would have weakened the claim, were frozen before
this ran.

\textbf{The control subspaces.} We fit ridge regressions from layer-$4$ activations to
the L1-normalized pitch-class histogram of the last $w$ tokens over $50{,}400$ positions
of the training split, for $w=16$ and $w=512$, the two windows of
\texttt{lr\_W16cat512}, the strongest note counter in the paper. $\lambda$ was chosen
from $\{10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1}\}$ on positions of the search-stage prompts and came out
at $10^{-3}$. The final-test prompts are used for neither the fit nor that choice.

The fit is good, which is what makes the control worth running. On the search prompts
$R^2$ is $0.654$ for $w=16$ and $0.781$ for $w=512$, and on a held-out split of the
fitting positions $0.631$ and $0.754$. The control is therefore a genuine encoding of
pitch-class frequency rather than a weak one.

\textbf{It barely overlaps $V$.} Only $5.6\%$ of $V$'s energy lies inside the
$22$-dimensional stack of both windows, against $0.047 \pm 0.003$ for random rank-$24$
subspaces, and all $22$ principal angles fall between $63.55^\circ$ and $89.40^\circ$
with none below $30^\circ$. Projecting the stack out of $V$ leaves $V$ at full rank $24$.

a probe trained on pooled positions, so a position-specific subspace remains untested as

rows were never aggregated, and the main text does not report them; we do that here.

Over the six bars the continuations reach, the one-shot replacement holds $0.687$ to $0.711$
of notes inside the target key against $0.575$ to $0.584$ for the same write into a
random subspace, separated at every bar by a prompt-paired Wilcoxon test after Holm
correction, with no visible decay. The sustained edit sits far above both, at $0.978$ to

not establish a persistent internal state: the model may be re-reading the key from the
notes it has already produced, which would reproduce this curve without anything being
held. Separating the two needs the token-splice control, which is outside this
submission's scope.

ones, and a cadence, a dominant-to-tonic bass motion into a complete tonic triad,

on both sides, are the values to read.

The second success condition bounds how surprised a reference model is. That is a
summary, and an edit could stay inside it while thinning the texture or dragging the
register. Measured against the unedited continuation of the same prompt, the
replacement moves one attribute of three: notes per bar fall by $0.595$
($r=-0.41$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=1.2\times10^{-3}$), while mean pitch ($-0.092$, $p=0.43$)
and register width ($-0.223$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.36$) do not move at all.

The displacement-matched control moves all three, and much further: notes per bar by
$-3.471$ with a rank-biserial effect of $-1.000$, mean pitch by $-0.484$, register width
by $+1.323$. Comparing the two directly on the same prompts, the replacement's change is
the smaller on every attribute (Holm-corrected). A write of that size is not gentle in
general; this one is.

The third arm settles what a legitimate key change looks like on these measures.
Transposing the prompt into the target key and continuing with no edit at all moves the
mean pitch by $+5.648$ semitones, a rank-biserial effect of $1.000$ since a
transposition must shift register, while leaving notes per bar ($-0.057$, $p=0.92$)
and register width ($-0.096$, $p=0.52$) untouched. The two interventions therefore have
opposite footprints: transposition moves the register and not the texture, the edit
moves the texture a little and not the register. Neither is a subset of the other, and
neither resembles the displacement-matched control, which moves all three.

Three attributes the audit was designed to cover cannot be measured on this corpus, and
we report them as unmeasurable rather than substituting others: the vocabulary has no
rest or chord token, and every note carries duration $8$ by construction, so the
duration distribution is a point mass and its divergence is identically zero in every
condition.

Minor prompts reach $0.495$ against $0.355$. Both halves of the measurement favour

The estimator is the larger half. The transposition reference is a continuation that is
in the target key by construction, so the rate it achieves is what this pipeline awards
a correct answer: $0.877$ in minor against $0.648$ in major. Read against its own mode's
ceiling, the edit reaches $0.564$ in minor and $0.548$ in major. The raw advantage of
$+0.139$ becomes $+0.015$. Essentially all of it is the estimator agreeing more readily
in minor, not the edit working better.

The pitch-in-scale rate is the smaller half. It allows minor the union of the natural,
harmonic and melodic forms, nine of twelve pitch classes, against seven for major,
so a minor continuation scores on notes that no single minor scale contains. Under the
harmonic minor the generator actually writes, the edited share falls from $0.957$ to
$0.903$, which is below major's $0.936$; the control falls from $0.764$ to $0.602$,
exactly major's $0.602$. The reported minor advantage on this measure is an artefact of
the definition, and the corrected comparison reverses its sign.

\section{The key subspace is not an artefact of the augmentation \normalfont\textit{(main Secs.~2.2, 5)}}
\label{sec:augmentation}
% 【日本語訳】調部分空間は増強の刷り跡なのか（本文4.4節、5節）
% クラス平均はどんな射影よりも前から五度圏を担っている。長調の間でのコサイン類似度と

% ことは、プローブがラベルを写していることを排除する。しかしそれは、訓練分布がこの表現を
% ほぼ不可避にしていることを排除しない。合成データでは、調は文字どおり生成器が標本を
% 引く潜在変数である。それについて語るのは本論文の公開チェックポイントの部分であり、
% そこでの記録は不完全である。MMT の訓練手続きは −5 から +6 半音のランダムな音高移動を

% チェックポイントと REMI のチェックポイントについては、移調増強が使われたかを公開情報
% から確定できなかったので、一般的な慣行を仮定するのではなく不明と書く。

The class means carry the circle of fifths before any projection: the rank correlation
between cosine similarity and circle-of-fifths distance among the major keys is $-0.936$
raw and $-0.961$ once the component common to all $24$ keys is removed. The natural
objection is that transposition augmentation put it there, since a training set built by
transposing every piece into every key teaches the twelve keys as twelve versions of one
thing.

Three of the six trained models had no augmentation, so the objection is testable
without re-searching anything: the same layer, the same construction of $V$, the same
statistic, and only the model changes. Augmented models give $-0.960 \pm 0.002$; models
trained without augmentation give $-0.963 \pm 0.003$. The two are indistinguishable, and
every one of the six falls between $-0.957$ and $-0.966$. The geometry is not a print of
the augmentation.

This does not settle the wider version of the objection, and we separate them. That the
vocabulary contains no key token rules out the probe copying a label. It does not rule
out the training distribution making this representation close to inevitable, and on
synthetic data the key is literally the latent variable the generator samples from. The
public checkpoints are the part of the paper that speaks to that, and there the record is
incomplete: MMT's training procedure states a random pitch shift of $-5$ to $+6$
semitones, so MMT is augmented and its cell does not answer the objection; for the
Anticipatory checkpoints and for the REMI checkpoint we could not establish from public
information whether transposition augmentation was used, and we write that as unknown
rather than assume the common practice.

% その分布のうち、モデルを放置したときにはプロンプトの調の音階に 0.9818 が乗っている。
% 部分空間を24のクラス平均の総平均で置き換えるとこれは 0.9085 に落ち、成分をそのまま
% 除去すると（h − P_V h）0.9069 になる。同じ rank のランダム部分空間を削除してもほとんど
% コストが無く、0.9782 である。調の除去はランダムな除去の約20倍のコストがかかり、その
% 効果は100本のプロンプト全体で満場一致である（順位双列相関 −1.000、p = 3.9×10⁻¹⁸ に
% 対し、ランダム統制は −0.396）。
% 2つのことがこれを必要性の実証にしないので、どちらもここに置く。低下は統計的に決定的で
% あり、音楽的には小さい。0.9069 でもモデルは圧倒的にプロンプトの調にあるので、調は V の
% 外側から大きく回復可能であるか、各位置でプロンプトから読み直されている。そして第2の
% 指標の向きは我々の予想と違った——誘導された調の事後分布のエントロピーは上がるのではなく
% わずかに下がり（1.936 から 1.901）、最も確率の高い調はプロンプトの 0.71 で変わらな
% かった。消去はモデルの、その調の音階への傾倒を緩めるが、モデルをどの調にいるか分からない
% 状態にはしない。
%

Every intervention so far writes a key in, which can only show that the subspace is
sufficient to set the key. Removing what it carries, and writing nothing back, asks
whether the model was using it. This is a forward pass at the bar-$9$ boundary, reading
the distribution over the next pitch; nothing is sampled.

Of that distribution, $0.9818$ sits on the prompt key's scale when the model is left
alone. Replacing the subspace with the grand mean of the $24$ class means drops it to
$0.9085$, and removing the component outright ($h - P_V h$) to $0.9069$; deleting an
equal-rank random subspace costs almost nothing, $0.9782$. The key removal costs about
twenty times what the random removal costs, and the effect is unanimous across the
hundred prompts (rank-biserial $-1.000$, $p = 3.9 \times 10^{-18}$, against $-0.396$
for the random control).

Two facts keep this from being a demonstration of necessity.
The drop is decisive statistically and small musically: at $0.9069$ the model is still
overwhelmingly in the prompt's key, so the key is largely recoverable from outside $V$
or re-read from the prompt at each position. And the direction of the second measure
was not what we expected, since the entropy of the induced key posterior fell slightly
($1.936$ to $1.901$) rather than rising, and the most probable key was unchanged for
$0.71$ of prompts. Erasure loosens the model's commitment to the key's scale; it does
not leave the model unsure which key it is in.

\label{sec:public-logits}

The main text shows on our own model that the edit changes the next-pitch distribution,
so it acts before any note is drawn. The same question can be put to a public
checkpoint without sampling. At the position the probe uses, the step whose next
token is a note, the distribution is folded over instruments to twelve pitch classes
and read as the log ratio of mass on the target key's scale to mass on the prompt
key's.

On AMT small over Bach, at the layer the sweep had already chosen, that ratio moves from
$-0.0239$ unedited to $-0.0096$ under the edit (rank-biserial $0.494$,
$p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0018$), while a dimension-matched random subspace does not move it at
all ($-0.0216$, $r = 0.004$, $p = 0.98$). The mechanism demonstrated on the synthetic
model is present in a public checkpoint, and one limitation the paper carried is
answered.

The size of the shift is not comparable across models. Our own model
separates the two keys by $0.695$ against $0.041$ for its control; here the separation
is $0.014$ of a log ratio. The claim this licenses is that the edit acts before
sampling in a public model too, not that it acts as strongly.

In REMI and MMT the edit reverses the sign of the ratio, so the target key's scale
ends up carrying more mass than the prompt key's before a single note is drawn. Every
edit-minus-random difference is paired by prompt and one-sided, with
$p \le 1.1\times10^{-11}$ in those two and $p = 3.4\times10^{-4}$ in AMT.

Two qualifications apply. In REMI and MMT the random subspace also moves the ratio
slightly against no edit ($+0.026$ and $+0.075$, both significant), so the pre-fixed
reading, that the ratio moves under the edit and not under the control, had to be
measured as the contrast it names rather than as two separate tests against no edit.

left as they were. The differences are twenty to thirty times the control's movement,
so a control that moves a little does not account for the edit. And the measurement
needed two implementation faults repaired first, since the pitch-class folding had been

separate pitch field. It now lives in each adapter, and AMT's published numbers
reproduce unchanged after that change, which is why the other two were run only
afterwards.

The DUR arm's gain over its control is $+0.1927$ in major (BCa $95\%$ CI
$[0.1618,0.2227]$, worst Holm-corrected $p=0.054$, $r$ $0.37$ to $1.00$) and
$+0.2064$ in minor ($[0.1809,0.2327]$, worst $p=5.3\times10^{-4}$, $r\ge0.89$),
while the BAR arm's is $-0.0064$ in major ($[-0.0191,0.0055]$) and $-0.0027$ in minor.
Its pitch-in-scale rate stays at the unedited level, $0.546$ against the control's $0.564$,
and its pass rate is exactly $1.000$ in both modes, which is what a write that
changes almost nothing looks like.

One objection remains. BAR covers only $0.0432$ of positions, so an
arm that edits fewer places could lose for reasons unrelated to where the key lives.
What the table settles is the comparison at equal coverage. DUR and PITCH cover the
same $0.4324$ of positions, and they score $0.2318$ and $0.0355$. Two arms of
identical size differ by a factor of six, so size is not what decides the outcome.
Every prompt in this corpus has the same token layout, $8$ bar marks, $16$ positions
and $80$ notes in $185$ tokens, which is why the shares are identical across modes.

So the three tokenizations agree on a mechanism rather than on a token name, and the
sentence in the main text can be read the same way in all of them. The causal site is
the step whose next token is a pitch, which is also the position the probe reads.

\section{The generation-free reading in minor \normalfont\textit{(main Secs.~4.3, 4.2)}}
\label{sec:nextpitch-minor}

% CONFIRMATORY_FREEZE の AMENDMENT 3 は短調の一式を登録したが、next-pitch テストだけは

% 適用し、何も標本抽出せずに次トークン分布を読む。音階マスクだけを、コード内で既に
% 凍結されている短調の定義 DIATONIC_MINOR_UNION に差し替えた。
% 結果は読み方1である。対数比は編集で 0.3094、対照で 0.0261 動き、12/12 で分離する
% （最悪 p=6.9e-15、r>=0.93）。書き込んだ調の音階上の質量も +0.1634 対 +0.0164 である。
% したがって「モデルが何も書く前に効く」という主張は両モードを覆う。
% ただし短調の動きは長調より小さい（0.3094 対 0.6953）。これは短調の音階マスクが
% 広く、編集前の質量が既に 0.7280（長調は 0.5471）あって上限が近いためである。
% 我々は比を主張せず、両方の値を出す。
%
The minor battery registered in \texttt{CONFIRMATORY\_FREEZE} excluded the next-pitch
test, because the script built major scales only, and it said a further amendment must
precede one. This is that run. The test is the major one unchanged. We append
\texttt{[BAR, POS\_1]} by teacher forcing, apply the edit at the bar-$9$ boundary, and
read the next-token distribution with nothing sampled. Only the scale mask changes, to
the minor definition already frozen in the code and used by the pitch-in-scale measure.

The first pre-registered reading holds. The natural-log ratio between the mass on the
target key's scale and the mass on the prompt key's scale rises by $0.3094$ under
the edit against $0.0261$ under the dimension-matched random subspace, and the difference
separates on all $12$ targets after Holm correction (worst $p=6.9\times10^{-15}$,
$r\ge0.93$). The probability mass on the target-key scale itself rises by $0.1634$
against $0.0164$. The claim that the effect precedes any feedback from the model's own
notes therefore covers both modes.

The minor shift is smaller than the major one, $0.3094$ against $0.6953$, and the
reason is visible in the same artifact. The minor mask is the union of the three minor
scales, so before any edit the mass on the target scale is already $0.7280$ against
$0.5471$ in major, which leaves the ratio less room to move. We report both numbers
and claim no ordering between the modes on this measure.

\textbf{Five findings weaken what the paper says.} The first is the most serious.
Across training seeds the edit reaches $0.3555$,
$0.2182$ and $0.1927$, a standard deviation of $0.0876$ where we had pre-registered
$0.03$ as the bar for calling the effect independent of the training run, and the run
reported in the main text is the strongest of the three (\S\ref{sec:seeds}). The
second: minor's advantage over major is the measurement, not the edit. Read against
each mode's own transposition ceiling the gap falls from $+0.139$ to $+0.015$, and the
pitch-in-scale definition that produced part of it reverses when minor is held to the seven
pitch classes the generator writes (\S\ref{sec:minor}). The third is a confound we
cannot close: the Bach cells were never length-matched, MMT hearing $240$ notes where
REMI hears $57$, and the re-score that would settle it needs continuations that were
not stored (\S\ref{sec:length}). The fourth is not new but is worth restating beside
the others. The probe margin over note counting is the $+0.071$ the main text reports
against the strongest counter, or $+0.105$ against the pre-specified one, which is real
but small.

The fifth concerns our own choice of target. Scaling the replacement continuously shows
it is a graded operation, rising monotonically up to full strength in both modes, but it
does not stop there. At $s=1.25$ the rate reaches $0.4245$ in major and $0.6182$ in
minor against the reported $0.3555$ and $0.4945$, with paired intervals excluding zero,
and in major that point also has a lower median likelihood change and a higher pass rate
than the replacement does (\S\ref{sec:steering}). Writing the class mean is therefore
not the best place to write, we have no account of why, and the frozen design is left
alone so every reported number still comes from $s=1$.

\textbf{Against that, several objections do not survive contact with the data.} The
edit is distance-independent while its control is not (\S\ref{sec:distance}); failures
land beside the target key rather than back at the prompt's (\S\ref{sec:landing});
the tonal centre moves and not merely the pitch collection, reaching about
three quarters of what a genuine transposition achieves on where the music comes to
rest (\S\ref{sec:tonic}); the finding holds under five key estimators in both modes, of
which the pre-registered one gives the lowest rate in major and the second lowest in
minor (\S\ref{sec:estimators}); the geometry and the
edit both survive training without augmentation (\S\ref{sec:augmentation},
\S\ref{sec:seeds}); the register and mean pitch are untouched (\S\ref{sec:selectivity});
resampling moves the rate by about a point (\S\ref{sec:gen-seed}); and the public
baseline is one measurement rather than three (\S\ref{sec:public}).

Four more objections joined that list after the sections above were first written, and
they are the strongest of them. The subspace is not the note counts in other
coordinates: a pitch-class subspace fitted from the same activations at $R^2$ $0.78$
overlaps $V$ no more than a random one does, writing through it is indistinguishable
from a random write of the same rank and displacement, and removing it from $V$ costs
nothing (\S\ref{sec:pitchclass}). The likelihood-change threshold is not what produces the
result, since re-scoring every condition from a threshold of $0.2$ out to no limit at
all never changes the ordering (\S\ref{sec:threshold}). The position asymmetry is not an
artefact of our tokenizer, because in two public schemes the pitch token is likewise
inert and the effect sits on the structural token before a pitch is chosen
(\S\ref{sec:public-positions}). And the read--use gap is not confined to raw probe
accuracy: in a public checkpoint the margin over note counting is high at layers where
editing does nothing, at both ends of the network (\S\ref{sec:public-layers}).

\textbf{Four things remain open, and none of them is closed by rewriting.} The Bach
length confound needs continuations we did not keep. The cadence detector is a
heuristic whose precision nobody has checked; its thirty saved detections are waiting
to be read. A one-shot edit persisting for six bars does not establish an internal
state, because the model may be re-reading the key from notes it has already produced,
and only the token-splice control separates those. And erasing the subspace costs
twenty times what an equal-rank random erasure costs but still leaves $0.9069$ of the
next-pitch mass in the prompt's key, so whatever the subspace carries, the key is
largely recoverable without it.

One boundary is worth stating because nothing in the paper tests it: every target is
one of the twelve keys of the prompt's own mode. No parallel-key replacement was attempted,
so nothing here shows that the mode can be changed at all.
