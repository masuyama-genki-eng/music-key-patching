\title{Does a Music Transformer Know What Key It Is In?\\
Causal Interventions on an Emergent Tonal Representation}

\name{Anonymous ICASSP 2027 submission}
\address{Paper ID: TBD}

\begin{document}
\ninept
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Musical attributes can be decoded from the activations of music generation
models, but decodability does not imply that generation \emph{uses} the decoded
representation. We test use directly: in Transformers trained on symbolic music
whose vocabulary holds absolute pitch and time only, we replace the
low-dimensional key component of the hidden state with another key's while the
musical prompt stays identical. On a fully held-out confirmatory set, with the
intervention layer, subspace, metrics and tests all frozen in advance, the edit
moves generation to the injected key where rank- and norm-matched random edits
do not (\TBD{held-out sweep running}), within a musicality budget frozen before
any edit ran. The effect precedes feedback from generated notes: before a single
new note is sampled, the edit shifts the next-pitch distribution toward the
injected key ($+0.26$ probability mass; matched random $+0.02$; $12/12$ keys,
Holm-corrected). The representation is broadly decodable across layers but
causally effective only in a narrow middle band, and edits restricted to
pitch-token positions are causally null---so the effect is not a trivial write
to pitch decisions, and where a state is readable is a weak guide to where it is
used. Probe and intervention transfer to a public checkpoint trained on real
music ($7.8\times$ the matched control, $12/12$ keys). We conclude that a music
Transformer computes a manipulable key representation that its generation
process causally consults.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
music generation, interpretability, causal intervention, tonality, probing
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% --- 背景 ---
Systems that compose music write convincingly tonal
pieces~\cite{hadjeres2017deepbach,huang2019musictransformer,copet2023musicgen}:
they settle into a home key, build tension away from it, and return. Probing
studies show that musical attributes, key among them, can be \emph{read out} of
such models' activations by trained classifiers~\cite{syntheory2024}. But a
signal can be decodable from a system's state without the system consulting it
when it acts~\cite{hewitt2019control,belinkov2022probing}. Whether a music
model's generation process \emph{uses} an internal key representation has not
been tested.

% --- 現状 ---
The test exists in other domains. A model trained only to predict Othello moves
tracks the board those moves imply, and editing that internal board changes
which moves it treats as legal~\cite{li2023othello,nanda2023emergent}; the same
causal standard has reached chess, mazes, and RL
environments~\cite{karvonen2024chess,maze2024,iris2026}. Music has probes, and
it has steering---inference-time interventions adapted from language
models~\cite{li2023iti,zou2023repe} that push attributes along learned
directions~\cite{smitin2024,musicrfm2026}---but steering shows that a direction
moves an attribute, not that the model maintains a representation whose
\emph{value} can be set: removed, replaced by another value, and then acted on.

% --- 提案 ---
We run that test with musical key as the variable. Key is defined outside the
model by theory and psychological measurement~\cite{krumhansl1982}, must be
computed by integrating pitch evidence over time (the vocabulary names no key),
and its scale gives an objective success measure with no learned judge. We
replace the key component of one layer's activations with a donor key's and ask
what the model composes next---under a design in which every choice (layer,
subspace, metrics, tests) was frozen before the confirmatory data were seen, an
intervention-free logit-level test rules out feedback from generated notes, and
token-type ablations rule out a trivial write to pitch decisions. We release
the testbed---leak-free tokenizer, corpus with exact per-token key labels,
frozen protocol---as a benchmark.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Data, models, protocol}
We need the true key at every note, so we generate training data. \textbf{D-SYN}
comes from a functional-harmony state machine (four-part voicing plus optional
melody; $200$k/$10$k/$10$k pieces of $278$--$508$ tokens; $0$--$3$ modulations
each). Every token carries a true key label $\kappa(t)$ ($24$ classes),
\textbf{which never reaches the model}: the vocabulary ($124$ types) holds only
\texttt{BAR}, \texttt{POS}, absolute-MIDI \texttt{PITCH}, \texttt{DUR} and
structural symbols, enforced by a unit test. \textbf{M-CTRL} is a GPT-2 style
decoder~\cite{vaswani2017attention,radford2019gpt2} ($8$ layers, $d{=}512$,
${\sim}25$M parameters), trained with and without transposition augmentation
$\times\,3$ seeds. \textbf{M-REF} shares the architecture but a different seed
and data slice; it only judges whether edited music still sounds like music, so
no model grades its own work. All models clear a preset quality gate
(validation top-$1$ $0.878$--$0.881$ \vs the $0.434$ of a most-frequent-token
predictor). Decision rules, thresholds and controls were frozen before
experiments ran; deviations are logged and negative results reported.

\subsection{Probing with controls}
Linear probes~\cite{alain2017probes,belinkov2022probing} map the residual
stream $h_\ell(t)$ to $\kappa(t)$, split by piece, read at uniformly sampled
positions. Three control families bound them: \textbf{C1} label permutations
(their score on true keys is the \emph{selectivity
floor}~\cite{hewitt2019control}, subtracted from every margin); \textbf{C2} an
untrained model; \textbf{C3} input-side baselines that name the key from the
notes alone---logistic regression on pitch-class histograms and
Krumhansl--Schmuckler (KS) matching~\cite{krumhansl1990,temperley1999ks} over
windows $W\in\{8,\dots,64\}$, extended in Sec.~\ref{sec:h1} to the full prefix.
The frozen rule \textbf{DR-H1}: the probe's selectivity-corrected $F_1$ must
beat the best C3 with a piece-level BCa~\cite{efron1987bca} $95\%$ CI excluding
zero.

\subsection{Intervention}
\label{sec:methodB}
From bar~$9$ of an $8$-bar key-stable prompt, a sustained edit replaces the key
component at one layer at every generated position, following activation
patching~\cite{geiger2021causal,meng2022rome}:
$h \leftarrow h - P_V h + P_V\,\mu_{\kappa^{*}}$, with $V$ an orthonormal key
subspace, $P_V{=}VV^{\!\top}$, and $\mu_{\kappa^{*}}$ the class-mean activation
of the donor key. \textbf{The prompt never changes.} $V$ is built three ways
(probe row space \textbf{V-PROBE}; class-mean span \textbf{V-MEAN}; optimized
\textbf{V-DAS}~\cite{geiger2024das}); four controls bound the result:
\textbf{K1}, a rank- and norm-matched random subspace (measured
$\|\Delta\|$ within $1\%$ of the edit's); \textbf{K2}, a sham edit that must
reproduce clean output bit for bit (it does, $100/100$, re-verified on every
prompt set); \textbf{K3}, another layer's subspace; \textbf{K4}, transposing
the prompt itself---the behavioral ceiling. Continuations are scored by the
\emph{target-key rate} ($\tkr$: KS estimate equals the injected key, reported
strictly) and the \emph{in-key rate} ($\ikr$: fraction of pitches in a given
scale, no estimator). A musicality budget $\ppl{=}0.613$---the $90$th percentile
of M-REF's perplexity rise over $7{,}989$ \emph{natural} modulations, frozen
before any edit---discards edits that damage the music more than a real
modulation; all headline rates are \emph{guarded}.

\subsection{Confirmatory design}
\label{sec:confirm}
Our first sweep compared $8$ layers $\times$ $3$ subspaces on $100$ prompts and
evaluated the best cell (V-PROBE, L$4$) on those same prompts---and those
prompts lie inside the probe's training pool. Holm correction covered the $12$
targets, not the selection. We therefore treat that sweep as
\emph{selection}, and add a \emph{confirmatory} phase: $100$ fresh prompts from
a region of the test split untouched by probe training and by every prior run;
layer, subspace, timing, targets, controls, guard, metrics, tests, seeds and
sample size all frozen in a version-controlled document committed before the
first confirmatory number existed. No layer or subspace search is repeated.
Identity cells (injected key equals the prompt's key) are excluded from the
primary analysis and reported as a sanity check. All intervention choices and
evaluation criteria were frozen before evaluating the held-out set.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The key is decodable beyond strong input baselines}
\label{sec:h1}
At L$4$ the probe reaches macro-$F_1$ $0.927$ against the best pre-registered
input baseline $0.790$ and a selectivity floor of $0.032$: corrected margin
$+0.105$ (BCa $95\%$ CI $[0.091,0.114]$), positive in layers $1$--$7$ of all six
trained models (DR-H1 $6/6$), negative at the raw embedding ($-0.20$), $0.243$
for an untrained model. Because the probe attends the full prefix while C3 saw
$W\le64$ tokens, we extended the baseline to the full prefix plus
recency-decayed and short${+}$long-concatenated variants: the window curve turns
over at $W{=}96$ ($0.801$) and falls to $0.752$ at $W{=}512$---under
modulations, stale evidence poisons a histogram---and against the strongest
variant ($0.824$) the margin remains positive, $+0.071$ $[0.056,0.081]$, with
all twelve size/seed verdicts unchanged. We say ``more linearly accessible than
these pitch-statistical baselines,'' not ``beyond all conceivable surface
functions'': hidden states are themselves functions of the input.

\subsection{Main causal result: held-out, pre-specified}
\label{sec:h3}
\textbf{Selection phase} (the original sweep; its prompts overlap probe
training): V-PROBE at L$4$ was the best in-budget cell, guarded strict $\tkr$
$0.378$ \vs its layer-matched K1 $0.075$ ($5.0\times$; $12/12$ targets,
Holm-corrected $p<10^{-4}$; guard pass $87\%$; V-DAS $0.213$; V-MEAN destroys
music at deep layers, in-budget ${\le}5\%$; $58\%$ of the K4 ceiling). Raw
pitch content agrees without the estimator: $\ikr_{\kappa^*}=0.937$ \vs
$\ikr_{\mathrm{src}}=0.646$.

\textbf{Confirmatory phase} (held-out prompts; everything frozen at commit
time; identity cells excluded): \TBD{held-out confirmatory sweep executing ---
per-target guarded $\tkr$, K1, rank-biserial $r$, Holm-corrected $p$, BCa CI,
guard rate, and identity sanity numbers land here}.

\textbf{Specificity.} The edit does not merely leave the source key; it lands
on the injected one. \TBD{confirmatory specificity: diagonal mass,
fifth-neighbor mass, source-retention, edit \vs K1}. In the selection-phase
data, success falls gently with circle-of-fifths distance ($0.395$ at one step,
$0.280$ at the tritone), as moving a key should.

\subsection{The effect precedes any generated note}
\label{sec:nextpitch}
A sustained edit leaves a loophole: perhaps the edit perturbs a few early notes
and the model then re-estimates the key from its own output. We close it at the
logit level. Each held-out prompt is extended by a teacher-forced, key-neutral
structural prefix (\texttt{BAR}, \texttt{POS\_1}); the edit is applied from the
bar-$9$ boundary; \emph{no token is ever sampled}. We read the next-token
distribution over pitch tokens and score the frozen metrics
$P_{\mathrm{target}}$ (probability mass diatonic to the injected key) and
$\Delta_{\mathrm{key}}=\log P_{\mathrm{target}}-\log P_{\mathrm{source}}$.
The edit shifts $\Delta_{\mathrm{key}}$ by $+0.695$ over clean; matched random
edits shift it $+0.041$. Target-scale mass rises $+0.255$ from a clean baseline
of $0.547$ (K1: $+0.017$). The separation holds in $12/12$ keys (Holm-corrected
worst $p{=}5{\times}10^{-16}$; rank-biserial $r\ge0.99$). \textbf{The
intervention changes the model's next musical decision before any
generated-note feedback can occur.}

\subsection{Where it is readable is not where it is used}
\label{sec:h5}
Two dissociations locate the effect. \emph{Across layers}: the probe saturates
broadly (macro-$F_1$ $0.920$--$0.929$ across L$2$--L$6$), but editing each
layer in turn and subtracting the matched control gives a single mid-network
peak ($0.03, 0.04, 0.19, 0.26, \mathbf{0.30}, 0.26, 0.23, 0.23$ for
L$0$--L$7$; best-vs-median CI $[0.038,0.109]$ excludes zero)---and near-zero
efficacy at L$0$--L$1$ where the probe already reads the key well
(Fig.~\ref{fig:layers}). The peak's index is not sacred---the second seed
replicates every verdict with its peak at L$2$---so we claim middle-layer
concentration, not a landmark layer. \emph{Across token types}: restricting
the write to \texttt{PITCH} positions is causally null (selection-phase
guarded $\tkr$ $0.077$, at the K1 floor, music untouched), while
\texttt{BAR}/\texttt{DUR} positions alone carry $69\%$ of the full effect and
no restriction improves the guard rate. \TBD{confirmatory token-type arms
(all / pitch / bar-dur) land here}. An edit that acted by trivially steering
pitch logits would show the opposite pattern. Where a representation is
linearly decodable is a weak guide to where it is causally used.

\subsection{A public model trained on real music}
\label{sec:wild}
We probe and edit the Anticipatory Music
Transformer~\cite{thickstun2023anticipatory} (\texttt{small}; trained on Lakh
MIDI~\cite{raffel2016lakh}, MetaMIDI~\cite{ens2021metamidi},
FMA~\cite{defferrard2017fma}) on $300$ Bach
chorales~\cite{hadjeres2017deepbach,huang2017coconet} with human local-key
labels~\cite{gotham2023wheninrome}. Probes here read at the positions where
the model is about to choose a pitch---the convention the (time, duration,
note) encoding forces, unlike the uniform sampling of Sec.~\ref{sec:h1}; the
supplementary shows the verdict inverts if one reads after the note instead.
Its probe beats the surface baselines
(corrected margin $+0.196$, CI $[0.132,0.260]$) where our same-shaped
synthetic-trained model does not ($+0.034$, CI crosses zero); the C3 baselines
land within $0.003$ of each other, confirming matched setups. We note the
comparison varies training data \emph{and} tokenizer and training procedure;
it isolates ``real-music training pipeline,'' not data alone. The intervention
transfers: with the budget re-frozen from natural chorale modulations and a
\emph{different} public checkpoint judging, the edit reaches $7.8\times$ the
matched control (guarded $0.479$ \vs $0.061$; $12/12$ targets; guard $99.7\%$;
$\ikr$ $0.890$ \vs $0.753$). Its causal peak (L$8$) again differs from its
probe peak (L$10$). One first-run finding did not survive its control and we
retract it: an apparent ``key prior'' (F$\sharp$ unreachable; success tracking
corpus key frequency, Spearman $+0.91$) was manufactured by estimating edit
targets from the same imbalanced corpus---F$\sharp$ never occurs, so its
target was a zero vector. Re-estimating from a key-balanced corpus lifts
F$\sharp$ to $0.617$ and collapses the correlation to a non-significant
$+0.41$: a portable caution for activation editing.

\subsection{What the intervention does not show}
\label{sec:limits}
Three bounded claims, each measured. \emph{Capacity}: across $0.5$M--$86$M
parameters next-token accuracy is flat, yet the smallest model has no
decodable key state and its edits wreck the music ($90\%$ out of budget);
manipulability peaks at $26$M (full sweep in the supplementary).
\emph{Persistence}: a one-shot installation shifts pitch content durably, but
splicing the installed bar's \emph{tokens} reproduces the entire effect---the
representation is re-estimated from evidence at every step, causally consulted
but not a persistent latent memory independent of emitted notes.
\emph{Geometry}: the twelve tonic directions arrange on a circle of fifths and
transposition acts as an approximately linear map, but not a cyclic group
(pre-registered H2b not supported; details in the supplementary).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}
Within this training condition, the evidence supports a precise claim: the
model computes an internal representation of musical key that is causally
consulted during generation---manipulable, target-specific, effective before
feedback, concentrated in middle layers and at structural token positions.
``World model'' is one interpretation of that bundle; we use it only here, and
only as interpretation. The gaps are informative: the edit reaches $58\%$ of
the behavioral ceiling, fails where the state is most readable (L$0$--L$1$,
\texttt{PITCH} positions), and K3 shows the middle-stack subspace is shared
rather than re-learned per depth. \emph{Bounds.} Major targets only (minor is
pre-registered but unrun). The KS estimator confuses fifth-related keys;
$\ikr$ triangulates it. The likelihood-based guard measures distributional
damage, not musicality itself; no listening test was run. The public-model
causal result is one checkpoint at one size. The confirmatory phase covers our
own model; the public-model intervention retains its original design (layer
chosen on disjoint prompts, budget frozen before evaluation, but no second
held-out round). Supplementary material carries the capacity sweep, geometry,
persistence, specificity matrices, per-seed variants, and the balanced
re-estimation study.

\section{Conclusion}
A music Transformer does not merely expose correlates of key to a probe. Under
a pre-registered, selection-corrected design, replacing a rank-$24$ component
of its hidden state moves composition to the injected key---before any
generated note can feed back, specifically toward the target, within a frozen
musicality budget, and reproducibly across seeds and on a public model trained
on real music. Decodability and causal use dissociate across layers and token
types; reading a representation is not evidence that the model consults it.
The testbed, labels, and frozen protocols are released as a benchmark.
